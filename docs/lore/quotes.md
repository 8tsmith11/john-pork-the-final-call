# Hard-hitting lines: the craft rules and a seed set

> Trevor asked for **very hard-hitting quotes**, with the craft researched, not guessed. Owner of
> the full line set: **gonman's agent** (Fable, task on the board). This file has the rules
> plus a seed set to calibrate against.

## What research says makes a line stick

1. **Distinctive words, common syntax.** Memorable movie quotes pair *unusual word combinations*
   with *ordinary sentence structure*, and they lean *general* (they travel out of context). Ad
   slogans that stuck share the same profile. (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al., ACL 2012.)
   → Plain grammar, one strange word choice, and a line that could be quoted at a stranger.
2. **Fluency feels true.** Rhyme, rhythm, and easy processing make sayings seem *more true* and
   easier to recall (the rhyme-as-reason / "Keats heuristic" effect). → Use it for slogans,
   creeds, and Continuity propaganda. Use it sparingly for sincere lines, because a too-polished
   line can sound like a slogan. (That's useful for Continuity, and dangerous for Pork.)
3. **Peaks and ends are what's remembered.** People judge an experience by its most intense moment
   and its ending (the peak-end rule). → Spend the best lines on **boss peaks and act endings**,
   not in filler conversation.
4. **Subtext beats statement.** On-the-nose lines drain tension. Characters should talk *around*
   the feeling, or play against it. → Grief at a funeral talks about the casserole.
5. **Setup → callback.** A line hits hardest when it re-uses something the player heard earlier,
   now with new weight (running jokes like "rookie Aster mistake" are our callback engine).
6. **Brevity and reversal.** The strongest lines are short and turn in their last few words.

## The rules for this game

- **One great line per scene, at most.** Scarcity is what lets them land.
- **The line belongs to the character.** Pork's lines sound like Pork (see `deep-lore.md` §3).
- **Never state the theme.** A line can *imply* "the edges are where the life is," but nobody
  says it.
- **Earn the quote.** Most great lines are callbacks or reversals of something said hours earlier.
