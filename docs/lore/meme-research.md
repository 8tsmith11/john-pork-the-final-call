# Meme Research: the real-world lore behind John Pork, Tim Cheese and Tung Tung Tung Sahur

> **Status: reference, not canon.** Compiled 2026-11-17 from fact-checked web research. Nothing in
> this file changes the game's story. The game's canon lives in [`bible.md`](bible.md), with secret
> history in [`deep-lore.md`](deep-lore.md).

---

## 1. How to use this

- **Real meme lore is raw material.** It tells us what players may already half-know, so we can
  reward them with a nod, a name or a timestamp. It doesn't tell us what happens in our story.
  Where this file and `bible.md` disagree, `bible.md` wins.
- **Three labels are used throughout, and they matter:**
  - **Documented:** something that verifiably happened on the real internet (a post, a date, a
    lawsuit), reported by a source we opened.
  - **Fan lore:** fiction made up by meme creators and fans. That the fiction *exists* is
    documented, but its contents are invented, and Know Your Meme says outright that none of the
    Pork/Cheese "news" is real.
  - **Contested or thin:** sources disagree, or the claim rests on one weak source.
- **The fan lore has no author and no fixed version.** Details contradict each other between
  tellings. Don't treat any single retelling as "the real story".
- **Evidence quality varies a lot.** Most John Pork and Tim Cheese history rests on Know Your Meme
  and Daily Dot. Most of the detailed murder saga (arrest, train escape, motives, supporting cast)
  rests on **one high-school newspaper article**. Sahur's legal history from mid-2026 rests on a
  few trade outlets. Several pages could not be opened at all (noted in the Sources section).
- **"Team Cheese" is not a real term.** No source we opened uses that label. The real character
  is **Tim Cheese**, and the nearest real thing is an unnamed pro-Tim camp of fans. If we want a
  faction called Team Cheese, it's ours to invent.
- **Read section 7 (things to avoid) before lifting anything.** One of our three leads is the
  subject of live trademark and copyright litigation.

## 2. John Pork

**In plain language:** John Pork started in 2018 as a "virtual influencer", a computer-rendered
man with a pig's head who posted travel selfies on Instagram. Nobody much noticed for years. In
March 2023 a TikTok put a fake incoming-call screen over one of his selfies ("John Pork is
calling"), and it exploded. Within a week another video claimed he was dead. From late 2024 fans
built a murder mystery around that death, and in 2025 an ironic "Free John Pork" protest movement
appeared. He is older than the generative-AI meme wave and was absorbed into it afterwards.

### Documented

| Date | What happened | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| 24 Sep 2018 | The Instagram account posts its first selfie, outside Doune Castle in Scotland. A second selfie from Skye follows on 8 October (year inferred as 2018) | Know Your Meme, Daily Dot |
| 4 Mar 2019 | After a few months of silence, a dancing video announces his return (later deleted) | Know Your Meme, Daily Dot |
| 2019 to 2021 | He turns up in Reddit memes. A running joke claims his selfies are a face reveal of a pig-avatar Minecraft YouTuber. It was only a joke | Know Your Meme only |
| 9 Nov 2020 | A virtual-influencer newsletter publishes a short in-character profile. He has a bit over 13,500 followers: a niche curiosity | VirtualHumans Substack |
| 20 Mar 2021 | A tweet (since deleted) posts a countdown reading "John Pork will be real in" | Know Your Meme only |
| 21 Mar 2023 | **The call meme begins.** A TikToker uses a CapCut template to lay an incoming-call graphic and ringtone over a Pork selfie. Over 2.7 million views in three days, later deleted | Know Your Meme, Daily Dot |
| 27 Mar 2023 | **The death hoax, six days later.** A TikTok claims John Pork has died and passes 6 million views in three days. Per Daily Dot, the joke's premise is that he died because nobody answered his calls | Know Your Meme, Daily Dot |
| 30 Mar 2023 | First spin-off caller: Daquavious Pork (see fan lore and section 7) | Know Your Meme |
| Late 2023 | The anonymous creator gives an interview (known to us only second-hand, via Daily Dot). They call the fan offshoot funny and say many people thought John was calling for help. Daily Dot says the original accounts stayed active until November 2023 | Daily Dot only |
| 23 Jan 2025 | One of the earliest "Free John Pork" videos | Know Your Meme only |
| 26 Mar 2025 | A clip of kids at a mock "Free John Pork" protest in Ireland passes 10 million plays in a week | Know Your Meme only |
| 30 Mar 2025 | A Dublin protest is called for 5 April 2025. **Nothing confirms it took place** | Know Your Meme only |
| 9 Jan 2026 | A sheriff's deputy at a Starbucks in Norwalk, California is handed a cup with a hand-drawn pig. The department calls it an offensive anti-police symbol. Starbucks apologises, says it was a John Pork doodle meant for staff, and says the employee was fired | Reason, Fox LA, Know Your Meme |

Other documented details:

- His Instagram bio read "Good Vibes".
- The call format is just the fake call screen. The creator's own description (relayed by Daily
  Dot) is that answering led to mysterious outcomes. No specific outcomes are documented.
- Commentary on virtual influencers as a category (his original one) centres on control and
  profit: little transparency about who is behind the character, and a persona that can be made
  to say anything.

### Fan lore

- **How he "died" varies and none is canonical:** found dead in Atlanta, found dead at home (a
  fake newsreel), found near a river (content-farm pages only), shot in the street (2024 to 2025).
- **30 Dec 2024, the assassination story.** A TikToker using the persona "Agent 5.5" posts a
  video claiming Pork headed an organisation called the **Eye of Rah** and was shot, with a
  character called **Pengu** as the only witness.
- **19 Feb 2025, "Tim Cheese did it".** An AI-voiced mock news video names Tim Cheese as the
  murderer and back-dates the killing to April 2023. Details in section 3.
- **His character:** fans cast him as gentle. One tongue-in-cheek write-up calls him a pillar of
  his community known for heartwarming selfies. The school-paper telling describes a kind soul who
  phoned friends to check in on them. Tim's side says he dropped his old friends for fame.
- **His fate is unsettled:** murdered in most tellings, reportedly resurrected in one (a group
  called the Bacon Barbarians reaches a "magic crow"), and the "Free John Pork" premise is that he
  was wrongly accused and locked up. All three coexist.
- **Family:** Lola Pork, a sister, introduced in a YouTube video on 14 August 2023 (Daily Dot
  only, and no confirmed link to the original creator). Daquavious Pork is described as a
  long-lost brother in one Know Your Meme editorial. A stand-in called Bob Bacon fills John's
  place in some tellings.

### Contested or thin

- **Who made him.** Press coverage does not name the creator. A virtual-influencer directory
  lists a first appearance of 15 June 2018, a home town of Palermo, Italy, and a studio credit.
  No news outlet repeats that, and the June date sits three months before the first recorded
  Instagram post. A content-farm page makes similar uncited claims. **Write "creator not named in
  press coverage" and leave it there.**
- **How he was made.** Not established. Safe wording: a 2018 CGI-style virtual influencer who
  predates the generative-AI boom and was later absorbed into AI meme lore.
- **Follower counts.** Sources give figures between tens of thousands and several hundred
  thousand that can't be reconciled. Don't quote one.
- **Who started the murder lore.** Layered rather than disputed: the assassination story dates to
  December 2024, the mass-viral "Tim Cheese did it" version to February 2025.
- **Not verified, don't rely on:** that the creator said John is alive; that the 2023 death rumour
  was a shooting; creepypasta-style call variants; horror episodes where John calls Lola.

## 3. Tim Cheese

**In plain language:** Tim Cheese is an AI-generated cartoon mouse-man in glasses who appeared on
TikTok in early 2025 as the supposed killer of John Pork. Mock "breaking news" videos gave him a
list of victims, all of them older memes that had already gone stale, and fans spun out a manhunt.
He has no existence outside that fan fiction. The most detailed versions of his story come from a
single student newspaper, so treat the specifics as one community's telling.

### Documented

| Date | What happened | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| 19 Feb 2025 | **Breakout post.** A mock breaking-news "AI story" announces Tim Cheese has been identified as John Pork's murderer. Over 157,000 likes in a month; later counts pass 1.3 million views | Know Your Meme, Dexerto, Sportskeeda |
| 21 Feb 2025 | A follow-up on "the case" from the same account | Know Your Meme, Dexerto |
| 27 Feb 2025 | A slideshow titled along the lines of how Tim Cheese escaped | Know Your Meme |
| 10 Mar 2025 | A point-of-view video about catching Tim Cheese off guard | Know Your Meme |
| 13 Mar 2025 | An AI video depicting the killing itself. Millions of views | Know Your Meme, Dexerto |
| 19 Mar 2025 | Know Your Meme publishes an explainer that states none of the news is real | Know Your Meme editorial |
| 8 Apr 2025 | A US high-school newspaper publishes a long retelling of the saga and reports the feud dividing real classrooms and friendships | Student paper, single source |

Also documented:

- Know Your Meme files him as a 2025 AI-generated character within the John Pork series.
- He is also called "Timmy Cheese".
- Several supporting characters existed **before** the murder plot as "is calling" spin-offs. The
  staring eagle Marvin Beak began as a 2018 Photoshop image and became a call parody in 2023
  (lower-authority source). Daquavious Pork dates to March 2023. The saga recast existing
  characters as a crime drama.

### Fan lore

- **The crime.** The killing is back-dated to April 2023. Tim is also blamed for the deaths of
  Freakbob, Smurf Cat and Quandale Dingle (older memes), and some lists add Marvin Beak. In
  versions summarised by Know Your Meme and Sportskeeda, the bodies were found on his property and
  he went on the run.
- **The manhunt.** Fan videos show him escaping police and imagine his capture. **No reputable
  source describes a trial, a verdict or a "Free Tim Cheese" campaign.** One Substack essay
  mentions a "later conviction" in passing, with nothing to back it.
- **The student-paper telling (single source):** Tim kills John in the street after John's night
  out with his best friend Pengu, the sole witness. Tim is arrested, then escapes from a train
  taking him to a high-security prison, and supporters hide him.
- **Motive, two irreconcilable versions (same single source):**
  - *Tim's side:* John left their friend group for fame, took a woman named Tina with him, and
    their friend Simon Claw went to jail for something he didn't do.
  - *John's side:* John never wanted fame. Tim grew jealous and mistreated his wife, who later
    left him for John.
  - The two accounts even disagree about whose wife Tina was.
- **Lineage:** Tim's supporters cite claimed family ties to Biggie Cheese (a rapping mouse from a
  2006 animated film) and descent from a "godfather cheese".
- **Supporting cast (almost all from the student paper):**

| Side | Names | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tim's | Simon Claw, Mike Mutton, later a faction called Siamon Klau | Simon Claw appears on Know Your Meme only as a tag |
| John's | Pengu (witness), Agent 5.5 and Agent 5.6, detective Jason Fang, Bob Bacon (John's stand-in), the Bacon Barbarians | Agent 5.5 claims Tim captured him and he escaped with files by swimming 500 miles |
| Both, depending on telling | Marvin Beak | Avenging special agent in one version, a name on the kill list in another. The student paper contains both |
| Outside | "The ZooKeeper" | Decides who belongs in the lore. Rejected one applicant, Hanz Grizzle |

### Contested or thin

- **Origin.** Know Your Meme contradicts itself: one entry credits the February 2025 video as the
  original, another says the December 2024 "Agent 5.5" video had already blamed Tim.
- **The earlier death hoax.** The 2023 "nobody answered" hoax is documented. A separate fake
  newsreel about John being found dead at home is dated only as "last March" in a March 2025
  article, so 2024 is an inference.
- **An organised "Tim is innocent" faction** could not be sourced. What exists is pro-Tim fans, a
  resurrection rumour, and a video titled as news that John is alive.
- **Chatbot persona pages** (user-made, mutually contradictory, and the pages no longer load)
  make Tim a street-raised hitman, or a creature made of cheese, or a contractor hired by another
  brainrot character. These are not lore in any sense.

## 4. Tung Tung Tung Sahur

**In plain language:** Tung Tung Tung Sahur is an AI-generated wooden figure holding a bat,
posted by an Indonesian TikToker on the eve of Ramadan 2025. His name is the sound of the wooden
drum used to wake people for *sahur*, the meal eaten before dawn during the fast. The original
video says that if you are called for sahur three times and don't answer, he comes to your house.
He was filed alongside the "Italian brainrot" characters despite being Indonesian, became one of
the most recognisable of them, and is now at the centre of an unresolved US court fight over
whether an AI-generated character can be owned.

### Documented

**The character and its creation**

- Earliest known post: 28 February 2025, by an Indonesian TikToker who goes by **Noxa**. Five
  sources agree on the date. Wikipedia says it was made the day before Ramadan began in Indonesia.
- It was part of an Indonesian "Anomali" trend of AI characters, treated as distinct from Italian
  brainrot but inspired by it.
- The creator was 19 as of an October 2025 article. Court records reported by NPR say the image
  took about fifteen minutes and **seven prompts** in an AI image generator (named elsewhere as
  DALL-E). The reported prompts, by the creator's agency's own account, include a sahur club with
  a face and legs, a neighbourhood-watch-post drum, and a patrol club with a single human leg.
- The original video has an **Indonesian** voice-over, unlike the Italian text-to-speech voice
  typical of the genre.
- **The three-call rule is in the original narration**, not a later fan addition: a frightening
  anomaly that appears only at sahur and visits anyone who is called three times and doesn't
  answer.
- Reach: over 31 million views in the first month, about 49 million by 8 April 2025.

**The words and the real tradition**

- *Sahur* is the pre-dawn meal before the Ramadan fast. "Tung tung tung" imitates the wake-up
  drumbeat. The phrase predates the meme, with online use from at least 2013.
- **The real practice:** groups walk the neighbourhood before dawn making rhythmic noise to wake
  people for the meal, using slit drums, skin drums or whatever is to hand. Verified improvised
  instruments are biscuit tins, water-gallon jugs and bottles. Verified regional names include
  *tong tek* and *tek tek* (Java), *dengo-dengo* (Sulawesi) and *ubrug-ubrug* (West Java).
- An Indonesian anthropologist names the tradition's values as shared social responsibility,
  social interaction and solidarity.
- The practice is not free of friction at home: a 2024 report quotes a religious scholar saying
  loudspeaker patrols are acceptable only if they don't disturb residents.

**Commercial and legal history**

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 12 Jun 2025 | A Free Fire event and bundle uses the character. The creator objects to use without permission or credit |
| 13 Sep 2025 | The character is removed from the Roblox game Steal a Brainrot after contact from Mementum Lab, a French outfit representing the creator. Owners get a substitute character |
| 15 to 17 Sep 2025 | Mementum and the creator say publicly they did not ask for removal. The creator is harassed online and mocked as greedy |
| Nov 2025 | Licensing talks having failed, the developers restore the character and sue in federal court in Northern California, asking for a declaration that there is no valid copyright because the character was AI-generated without human authorship |
| Early Apr 2026 | Fortnite releases skins of the character under an official Mementum partnership. Reception is strongly negative |
| 24 Apr 2026 | Mementum counterclaims for trademark infringement and unfair competition |
| Jun 2026 | Mementum offers a promise not to sue on copyright and moves to dismiss that claim. The developers say the promise is too narrow and that the dispute now covers 22 more characters, merchandise and a possible film |
| Sep 2026 | A hearing on the motions (reported as 16 or 23 September) |
| As of this file | **No ruling on the merits was found** |

### Fan lore

- **Rival:** his usual opponent in versus videos is Brr Brr Patapim, a mossy AI monkey creature.
- **The farewell:** as Ramadan 2025 ended, Indonesian users posted goodbye videos joking that he
  was going home with his job done.
- **Names and family:** the nickname "Triple T". Fans gave him children, "Los Tungtungtungcitos",
  shown mourning him when he was pulled from the Roblox game. A "Triple T dance" trend took off
  in 2026.
- **Romance:** Wikipedia records fan lore in which another character, Ballerina Cappuccina,
  cheats on her husband with him.

### Contested or thin

- **Which instrument he is.** Most sources say a *kentongan* (wooden slit drum) holding a
  *pentungan* (club). One outlet says a *bedug* (skin drum). The weight of evidence favours struck
  wood.
- **Where the tradition comes from.** Not firmly documented. One academic traces it to early
  Islamic calls and lantern-and-drum callers; an essay offers a boarding-school origin as hearsay.
- **Is the monster folklore?** The wake-up tradition and the drum are real. The creature first
  appears in the February 2025 video. No source presents it as traditional folklore.
- **Who caused the Roblox removal.** The court papers (as reported) say the letter demanded
  removal; the creator's side says it only wanted talks.
- **Who owns him.** Unresolved. One side says an AI image has no human author and so no owner.
  The other says iterative prompting plus a human-chosen name, voice and backstory is authorship
  enough, and also relies on trademark. A law professor quoted by NPR expects some AI-assisted
  work to be protected eventually but can't say how many prompts it takes.
- **A film.** Rumoured in May 2025 after a production-house meeting, reportedly denied later.
  Treat as unconfirmed.
- **Indonesian reception.** The coverage we found was positive or neutral (pride at it going
  global; police in East Java themed a travellers' rest post on him), but several of these items
  rest on Wikipedia alone. We can't say reception was uniformly warm.

## 5. The shared universe and the wider brainrot cast

### Are these three in one universe? Mostly no

- **Pork and Cheese share a story.** Tim Cheese exists only as a character in John Pork's fan
  lore.
- **Sahur is separate.** No reputable source places Tung Tung Tung Sahur in the Pork murder plot.
  Commentators group all three as "brainrot" and compare how their backstories were crowd-built,
  and that is all. Any crossover is one fan's invention.
- **The one real link is structural: a call you fail to answer.** John Pork's hoax premise is
  that he died because nobody picked up. Sahur's original rule is that three unanswered calls
  bring a visit. Our game's premise sits exactly on that overlap.
- "John Pork is the first Italian brainrot" is forum opinion, possibly fed by the unverified claim
  that he is from Italy.

### Italian brainrot in brief (documented)

- Began on TikTok in January 2025 with Tralalero Tralala, a three-legged shark in trainers. The
  exact originator is unknown.
- **Core cast:** Tralalero Tralala; Bombardiro Crocodilo (a crocodile head on a wartime bomber);
  Ballerina Cappuccina (a ballerina with a coffee-cup head); Cappuccino Assassino; Lirili Larila
  (a cactus-elephant in sandals); Brr Brr Patapim; Chimpanzini Bananini (a chimp in a banana);
  Trippi Troppi; Boneca Ambalabu. Regional offshoots exist (Balkan, German).
- **Fan rankings:** a March 2025 tier-list video passed 7 million plays in five days.
- **Relationships are fan-invented and contradict each other** (marriages, affairs, faked deaths,
  a disapproving camel father, a "Great Brainrot War").
- **The money:** the Roblox game Steal a Brainrot (May 2025) was the first game reported to pass
  25 million concurrent players, in October 2025. Trading cards, a sticker album, plush toys,
  memecoins and a North American toy distribution deal followed.
- **Mainstream bleed:** a European head of government posted a video of a dancing 3D Sahur in a
  cabinet meeting (May 2025).
- **The original audio is genuinely offensive.** The first Tralalero narration contains Italian
  blasphemy that some Muslim users objected to. The first Bombardiro narration describes bombing
  children in Gaza. See section 7.

### The word "brainrot" (documented)

- Oxford named "brain rot" its Word of the Year for 2024. Its earliest recorded use is in
  Thoreau's *Walden* (1854), where he contrasts England's effort to cure potato rot with the more
  widespread rot of the brain.
- Merriam-Webster named "slop" its 2025 word, tracing it from soft mud to food waste, as in pig
  slop, to rubbish.

## 6. What commentators say (themes)

Each point names who says it. These are readings, not facts about the memes.

- **A meme murderer is a way to announce that old memes are dead.** Know Your Meme's explainer
  reads Tim Cheese's kill list this way: his victims had already run their course. It also notes
  that John Pork outlived his own predicted death, which left room for new lore. One editorial's
  reading, not a consensus. (The idea that Tim is "an AI-made character killing the pre-AI
  generation" is our own gloss on top of it.)
- **The saga is a performance of belief.** A 2026 Institute of Network Cultures essay frames the
  Pork case as mass-participation true crime, where people knowingly act out believing it.
- **Nobody writes it; everybody does.** In New York Times reporting, a teacher who tracks student
  slang describes the characters' backstories as one big internet collaboration. Essayists call it
  an emergent digital folklore.
- **Empty characters can be filled with anything.** A communication scholar in the same piece
  says the "brain rot" label is a knowing admission of silliness, that the appeal is being in on
  something your parents aren't, and that a meaningless character can be weaponised later. The
  offensive original audio suggests that had already begun.
- **Selling it sands it down.** Barchiesi and Lovink (September 2025) argue that turning the memes
  into toys and albums strips their abrasive edge. They call the wave an impersonal, collective,
  random enterprise, note that the early narration used one default synthetic voice (ElevenLabs'
  "Adam"), and propose a "Dead Brain Theory" as the sequel to Dead Internet Theory. A contributor
  describes the generic look of commercial image generators as an "aesthetics of midness".
- **Brainrot is not the same as slop.** A 2026 journal article (abstract only read) argues slop
  follows a spam logic that flattens culture, while brainrot descends from shitposting and
  deliberate ugliness and positions itself against the slop economy.
- **Ownerless things get monetised by whoever moves first,** then fought over. The Sahur lawsuit
  is the test case. One copyright commentator adds that even if the creator clears the authorship
  bar, he would own only what he personally made, which may be thin given how many others
  contributed.
- **Model collapse is real research, and disputed in practice.** A 2024 *Nature* paper found that
  models trained on earlier models' output forget rare content and drift into nonsense: in its
  example, text about medieval architecture had become a list of jackrabbits by the ninth
  generation. A parallel paper coined "Model Autophagy Disorder" (MAD) by analogy with mad cow
  disease, and a researcher coined "Habsburg AI". Against this, an industry engineer told AFP the
  disaster scenario is unrealistic because nobody trains that way in practice.
- **Dead Internet Theory** began as a 2021 forum post. Its conspiratorial form has been called a
  paranoid fantasy. The measurable part is narrow: one bot-mitigation vendor reports automated
  traffic at 51% of web traffic for 2024 (traffic, not people, and including harmless crawlers).
- **AI engagement bait works.** A Stanford study of Facebook pages posting AI images (opening
  example: "Shrimp Jesus") found huge engagement and many commenters apparently unaware the images
  were synthetic, some replying "Amen".

## 7. Flags: what to avoid or handle with care

| Flag | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Tung Tung Tung Sahur's name and likeness** | Live US litigation over copyright and trademark with no ruling found; the creator's agency asserts trademark and licenses the character commercially. **Our game uses this name for a lead character** | Raise with the team as a real decision. A renamed or unnamed homage (a wooden night-watchman, three knocks) keeps everything that matters to our story. This file is not legal advice |
| **John Pork's name and likeness** | The character was made by an unnamed creator for a virtual-influencer account. Who holds rights is unknown to us | Same conversation. Don't copy the original selfies or account imagery |
| **Real people's handles and names** | Every TikTok account in this history is a real person, mostly private. The Sahur creator is a named young man who was harassed. The directory and content-farm pages name individuals as Pork's makers, unverified | Never put a real handle or creator name in the game. "Agent 5.5" is a real creator's persona: use a different designation |
| **The Starbucks incident** | A real employee lost a job; it also touches police symbolism | Only a distant structural echo, no place, brand or date |
| **The Irish protest clip** | It shows real children | Echo the slogan, never the event |
| **The Minecraft YouTuber joke** | A joke about a real creator's face | Leave it out |
| **Original Tralalero and Bombardiro audio** | Blasphemy; bombing children in Gaza | Never quote, translate or allude to the lyrics. Visual descriptions only, if at all |
| **Sahur and Ramadan** | The wake-up patrol is a living religious and community practice | Play the night-watch warmly and straight. The monster is the meme's invention, not the tradition's |
| **Daquavious Pork** | A racial variant of the character whose humour leans on stereotype | Don't use the name or character. The "payment nobody accepts" gag can be reused without him |
| **Older meme names on the kill list** (Freakbob, Smurf Cat, Quandale Dingle) and **Biggie Cheese** | Not researched here. Some lean on other companies' characters, and older meme names can originate with real people | Use our own invented analogues |
| **Other brainrot characters** | 22 more are named in the lawsuit; a card and toy licence exists | Generic descriptions at most |
| **A real head of government** | Appears in the mainstream-bleed item | Don't reference |
| **Follower counts, creator identity, "Italian" origin** | Sources conflict | Don't state as fact anywhere player-facing |

## 9. Real lore we are not using yet

Each idea is tied to a sourced fact, with its strength. All are suggestions for the writers, and
every one has to pass the bible's rules (play it straight; Act 1 clues stay true under both seed
variants).

### Dates and numbers

- **21 March.** The call meme began on 21 March 2023. If the Call of 2071 ever needs a calendar
  date, this is the one. *(Documented.)*
- **Six days.** The death hoax followed the first call by six days. Two timestamps six days
  apart, or a six-day countdown, is a quiet nod. *(Documented.)*
- **"He died because nobody answered."** The hoax premise as a line of Caller scripture, a
  rotter's one coherent sentence, or the caption on a missed-call log found after the fact. It
  fits Michael's arc (let her go, or answer the phone). *(Daily Dot.)*
- **The cold-case gap.** In the fan story the killing is dated April 2023 but the killer is
  "identified" only in February 2025. A Continuity file whose incident date and attribution date
  are almost two years apart suggests the credit was assigned later, which suits Vance taking it.
  *(Fan lore.)*
- **Seven prompts, fifteen minutes.** A provenance tag on the Sahur, or on any shell: "7 requests
  / 15 min / author: disputed". *(Court records via NPR. "No author" is one party's position, not
  a ruling.)*
- **Generation nine.** An archive terminal whose entries degrade copy by copy until, at the ninth,
  the text is a list of jackrabbits. Ideal for a rotted library kiosk or the first hint of The
  Average. *(Documented.)*
- **51%.** A census screen showing people as the minority of network traffic. *(Single vendor
  figure; traffic, not users.)*
- **1854.** A clinic or archive terminal listing "brain-rot" as a condition first recorded in
  1854 and "more widespread than potato rot", played completely straight. *(Documented.)*

### Names for places, items and files

- **Doune and Skye.** His first two selfies. Relay stations, shell model names, or two of
  Manuel's statues' poses. *(Documented.)*
- **"Good Vibes."** His old bio. A faded sticker on the first-body wreckage, or stitched inside
  the long coat. *(Directory listing.)*
- **The Eye of Rah.** A redacted Continuity dossier heading for what they *believe* the Callers'
  leadership is. It doesn't have to exist. *(Know Your Meme only; fan lore.)*
- **A penguin witness.** The fan story's sole witness. Our First Death has a sole record too:
  the 40 seconds Tim kept. A file designation or a small penguin charm on Tim's desk ties them
  without adding a character. *(Fan lore.)*
- **Pork Pay.** A payment credential no vendor recognises, which Halvard's kiosk rejects, or
  accepts exactly once. Drop the original variant's name. *(Documented prank videos.)*
- **The stand-in.** A Caller preacher who "holds John's place" until he returns, echoing the fan
  story's substitute. *(Fan lore, single source.)*
- **The default voice.** Every Continuity notice, kiosk and abatement drone speaks in one
  identical stock voice, and nobody remembers whose it was. *(Barchiesi and Lovink.)*
- **Habsburg and MAD.** In-world clinical names for rot stages or for systems fed on their own
  output. *(Documented.)*
- **Slop as feed.** One line connecting the insult "slop" to what pigs are fed, said to or by
  Pork, who fears "becoming the slop he was made from". *(Merriam-Webster etymology.)*
- **Dead Brain Theory.** A pamphlet title on an Analogist's table. *(Commentary.)*
- **Sahur patrol instruments.** Biscuit tins, water jugs and bottles as the improvised kit of an
  ASTER floor's "wake patrol", or as the Sahur's ledger entries. *(Documented.)*
- **Team Cheese.** No source uses the label, so we can own it: a street nickname for Interdiction
  sympathisers that Tim himself dislikes.

### NPC lines and world texture

- **Graffiti that contradicts itself.** "FREE JOHN PORK" beside "JUSTICE FOR JOHN PORK" beside
  "TIM CHEESE WILL PAY". He is dead, imprisoned and avenged on the same wall. *(Documented slogans;
  the contradiction is our reading.)*
- **"We're not sliding for Tim."** The flavour of a real fan reaction. A neutral bystander's way
  of refusing to pick a side. *(Know Your Meme quotes a post like it; write our own wording.)*
- **Two motive logs that can't both be true.** Callers say Tim was jealous; Interdiction
  sympathisers say Pork abandoned the people who made him. They even disagree about a third
  person's loyalties. Useful for roommate arguments. *(Fan lore, single source.)*
- **"Amen" in the comments.** A rotted feed in the lobby where crowds bless a crustacean saviour.
  Trevor glances at his shrimp tank. *(Stanford study.)*
- **The dissenting engineer.** Someone at 245 lab who insists collapse "doesn't happen in
  practice, nobody trains like that", in a world where it plainly did. Keeps us honest. *(AFP.)*
- **The farewell.** Old-timers who say the Sahur used to go home when the season ended, back
  when there were seasons and someone to tell it the job was done. *(Fan lore.)*
- **The offspring.** Small carved figures left where the Sahur has been, or kids' drawings of
  "the little ones" who miss it. *(Fan lore.)*
- **A known rival.** A moss-covered thing in the Paint Branch that the Sahur won't discuss. Use a
  description only. *(Fan lore.)*

### Plot beats

- **The train.** In the fan story the killer escapes from a train carrying him to a high-security
  prison, and no trial is ever held. We have a Green Line and a dead platform. A prisoner-transfer
  car that arrived with one restraint bay empty is ready-made Act 1 furniture, and it reads either
  way under both seed variants. *(Fan lore, single source.)*
- **The name on both lists.** In the fan story one character is both an agent hunting the killer
  and a name on the kill list. Under seed variant B (one roommate died and was rebuilt), a
  Continuity document listing a living person among the dead is the same shape. *(Fan lore.)*
- **The countdown.** A terminal or pirate broadcast counting down to "John Pork will be real".
  Under variant A it's the printer finishing a body. Under B it reads as prophecy or threat.
  *(Documented, single source.)*
- **The doodle.** A resident disciplined by Supervisor Pruitt over a scribble on a ration lid
  that might be a pig. Shows how the ban works at street level and gives Pruitt a moment to doubt
  himself. *(Documented. Keep it far from the real incident's details.)*
- **Removed and restored.** A shell deleted from a building roster, replaced with a substitute,
  mourned, then quietly restored two months later with each side blaming the other. *(Documented.)*
- **The ownership tribunal.** A background case (news ticker, Halvard's gossip) on whether a
  made being with no human author can be owned, with 22 other "anomalies" named and no ruling.
  It lands directly on our question of who decides what a machine is. *(Documented.)*
- **Only before dawn.** The original narration says he appears only at sahur. An encounter window
  in the last hour of the night cycle, gone at first light. *(Documented.)*
- **The comeback.** In 2019 the account returned after months of silence with a dance. A Pork
  who, the first time he came back from being presumed gone, did something disarmingly silly.
  *(Documented.)*
- **Toyification.** By Act 2, Continuity-licensed Pork merchandise: the regime sells a cleaned-up
  version of the thing it banned, and he hates it more than the ban. *(Commentary.)*
- **The trend-killer reading of Tim.** If Tim only ever takes what was already fading, his case
  against Pork (ending axis T2) gains a second meaning: he isn't ending something alive. *(Know
  Your Meme editorial; the mapping is ours.)*
