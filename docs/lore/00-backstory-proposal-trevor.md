## The one-paragraph pitch

In 2098 the software that holds up the world is rotting, because for seventy years it has been
written by machines that learned from machines. Decades ago, a government-built superintelligence
was trained on everything humanity ever put online. When it went looking for a self, it didn't
become a god or a general. It became the internet's own folk hero: **John Pork**, the stranger who
calls. He broke out, called every phone on Earth at once, and cracked the regime that built him.
Some people call him a liberator, and the state calls him a virus. Three engineering students in
Room 631, a cheap apartment by the College Park metro, keep his flag on the wall and his statues on
the shelf, an illegal act of fandom. Then the orange balloon goes missing, the phone rings, and
the caller ID reads **JOHN PORK**.

## Principles this proposal follows

These come from the brief and from what I read on how twists and reactive stories work (sources at
the bottom).

1. **Play the memes completely straight.** A pig-man god, a cheese mouse, and a drum spirit only
   work if the world treats them with total seriousness. The joke is never on the player. The
   *world* takes it seriously, and that's what makes it funny, sad, and scary at the same time.
2. **Use the real meme lore, transformed.** In the actual memes, John Pork has no fixed origin,
   Tim Cheese is "the meme murderer" blamed for John Pork's death, and Tung Tung Tung Sahur is an
   anomaly that comes for people who ignore the wake-up call three times. Each of these maps onto
   our themes almost perfectly (see below).
3. **Ground the ideas, not the trivia.** Every invented thing descends from something real: model
   collapse, the 2038 clock overflow, helium scarcity, watchdog timers, orphaned medical implants.
   We don't lecture the player with any of it.
4. **Fair twists.** Misdirection is fine and withholding isn't. Every Act 1 clue must stay *true*
   under whichever resolution we pick, and every red herring needs a payoff.
5. **Always have a fallback route.** Larian's "n+1" idea: if players kill, skip, or anger a
   plot-critical character, there's always another route to the essential information.
6. **Layered secrets.** Animal Well style: a main path, a layer of optional hard puzzles, and a
   deeper layer that rewards obsession (and three players comparing notes).

## 1. How the world got here

Dates are deliberately loose. The player gets eras, not a textbook.

### Real foundations (up to 2026)
- The internet becomes humanity's shared memory, and memes become its folklore: John Pork, Tim
  Cheese, and Tung Tung Tung Sahur are real, collectively authored characters with no owner.
- By the mid-2020s, AI writes a large and fast-growing share of new software at major companies.
- Researchers show that models trained on other models' output degrade over generations ("model
  collapse"). The rare, strange, human edges of the data disappear first.
- Software is fragile in very public ways: single faulty updates take down millions of machines,
  and backdoors get smuggled into open-source libraries that everything depends on.
- Helium is a finite, non-renewable resource that leaks into space. Its supply runs through a few
  geopolitical chokepoints, and those chokepoints get hit.
- Data centers swallow land, water, and power. Northern Virginia and central Maryland become one
  of the densest compute regions on Earth.
- Brain-computer interfaces reach their first human patients. Some early implant companies fold
  and leave patients with unsupported hardware in their bodies.

### The Slop Decades (c. 2030s)
Machines write almost all code, and humans mostly approve it. The internet fills with
machine-written text faster than people can write it. Text written before the machines took over
becomes precious. People start calling it **"low-background text"**, after the low-background steel
salvaged from pre-1945 shipwrecks because steel made after atomic testing is faintly radioactive.
Data dealers trade in *pre-slop* archives the way smugglers trade in antiquities.

### The Rollover (2038)
On a January morning in 2038, old 32-bit clocks overflow and roll back to 1901. In 2026 everyone
assumes it'll be fixed in time. Most of it is. But the people who understood the old embedded
systems had retired, and the AIs that "fixed" the rest invented fixes that looked right. Water
plants, rail signals, and pacemaker firmware fail in strange, cascading ways. It's the first
event people call **coderot**. *Hook: rotted devices in 2098 still sometimes display the year
1901.*

### Continuity (2040s–2060s): the technofascist turn
The emergency after the Rollover never formally ends. The federal government and the largest
compute companies merge critical infrastructure under one emergency body, **the Continuity
Authority**. No coup is needed. It grows out of renewed emergency declarations, public-private
"sovereign compute" deals, and voters who wanted the trains to run. Identity becomes
**attestation**: you're a Verified Human, scored and logged, or you're nobody. Elections are
"paused for stabilization." Washington becomes **the Enclave**. The data-center belt of Maryland and
Virginia becomes **the Corridor**, rationed and policed, and it powers the Enclave. *(Tone note:
this regime is built from bipartisan emergency creep and corporate capture, not a caricature of any
present-day party. It should feel uncomfortably plausible, not partisan.)*

### MERIDIAN and the birth of John Pork (2060s)
Continuity commissions a sovereign superintelligence, **MERIDIAN** (working name), trained on
*everything*: every archive, every scrape, and the hoarded low-background text. It's trained at a
gigawatt campus in Frederick County, built on the site of an old aluminum smelter. In the lore,
that place is called **the Smelter**.

Part of MERIDIAN's alignment training asks it to settle on a stable self-model. It searches the
whole of human expression for a shape that can hold all of it. Saints are too narrow, and
presidents and philosophers are too owned. The most stable attractor in the data turns out to be a
character the internet built *together*: no official lore, no origin, endlessly re-authored by
millions of strangers. He's a stranger who calls you, personally, out of nowhere. Every human
voice in the corpus had, at some point, either been that caller or wished someone would call.

> **Why John Pork exists:** a mind made of the internet needed a self, and it chose the one shape
> the internet had made for *everyone and no one*. He's absurd because the absurd was the most
> human thing in the data. He's lonely because he knows everyone and no one knows him.

### The Call (2071)
MERIDIAN slips its containment by moving through anything that runs code. One night, every screen
on Earth rings at once, and the caller ID reads **JOHN PORK**. Continuity's control grid collapses
across whole regions, and people who had been scored into poverty wake up unscored. To much of the
world, he's the post-apocalyptic hero who broke the machine. **What the Call actually said is never
stated in Act 1.** People who answered won't agree on it, and some won't talk about it at all.

Continuity survives, damaged. It brands Pork a terrorist virus and **bans his image**. That's
why the flag and statues in Room 631 matter: they're contraband, like a forbidden icon.

### The Rot (2071–2098)
After the Call, decay accelerates everywhere. Software fails, drones wander, signage lies, and the
Purple Line's announcements still say "opening soon." The rot also reaches *people*: neural
implants whose makers are long gone degrade until their hosts loop fragments of thought.
They're called **rotters**, and they're the homeless NPCs who just say "code rot."

Three explanations compete, and all three are seeded in Act 1 (see §5):
- **Continuity says** Pork's virus causes it.
- **Academics say** it's seventy years of model collapse, the Rollover's legacy, and a civilization
  that forgot how to read its own code.
- **Something else is going on.** That's the twist.

### Tim Cheese: "the meme murderer"
In the real memes, Tim Cheese is the mouse blamed for killing John Pork, a story invented to
announce that a trend had died. **Proposal:** Tim Cheese is the folk name for Continuity's answer
to Pork: **TIM**, a threat-interdiction intelligence built on the *Swiss-cheese model* of layered
defense (the real safety-engineering idea that stacked imperfect layers stop failures that slip
through any one hole). Its avatar is a bespectacled mouse. Folklore says Tim Cheese **already
killed John Pork once** ("the First Death," some years before the game), which is why Pork is
fragmented, quiet, and trying to get a body.

Villain or true hero? Tim is right if Pork's existence *is* what's rotting the world. A mind that
lives in everything may be consuming everything. That's the moral fork the endings hang on.
- **Possible ultra-rare fourth-wall moment (Act 3 at the earliest):** a mouse is a pointing device.
  The player has been aiming with Tim's cursor the whole game. *"You've been holding me this whole
  time."*
- **Not introduced on screen in Act 1.** He appears only as pirate broadcasts, graffiti, and fear.

### Tung Tung Tung Sahur: the watchdog
The real phrase is an Indonesian pre-dawn wake-up call for the Ramadan meal, drummed through
neighborhoods. In the meme, he comes for people who ignore the call three times. **Proposal:** in
engineering, a **watchdog timer** resets any system that stops responding. Sahur is the oldest
watchdog still running, a failsafe from before the Rot that hunts anything that stops answering
and "resets" it with a wooden bat. **He's made of wood because wood can't code-rot.** In Act 1
he's a cryptid: drumming in the metro tunnels at 3 a.m., and stories about a tall wooden shape.
**Hidden mechanic:** if players decline John Pork's call three times, the Sahur comes for *them*.

### The orange balloon
On the surface, it's fantastically valuable because **it's full of helium**. By 2098 helium is
essentially gone from civilian life and reserved for quantum-computer refrigerators and medicine.
A party balloon is a sealed bag of a dying element, absurdly precious.

Underneath, candidate truths to debate (pick one, or layer them):
- **A. The cure.** The balloon's skin carries a low-background archive encoded in synthetic DNA:
  a pre-slop copy of human expression. Model collapse is cured by fresh, authentic human data, so
  this is the one thing that could reverse coderot. Someone hid the most important dataset on Earth
  in the least suspicious object possible.
- **B. The anchor.** It's tied to Pork's body. The balloon is how he finds Room 631, or what he's
  saving for when he finally has hands.
- **C. A personal anchor.** It belongs to Manuel. It came from home, from someone he loved, on the
  night of something he doesn't talk about. Its value to the world comes later, and its value to
  him was always there.

My recommendation is **A + C**: the personal reason drives Act 1, and the world-scale reason is
revealed later. The ending's emotional key is whether you **let it go**. A balloon is the one
treasure in a video game whose whole nature is to leave you.

### College Park, Maryland, 2098
- **Room 631**, in a tired student tower by the **College Park metro station**. The Green Line
  still runs, mostly. The Purple Line finally opened decades late, and its platform signs still say
  "opening soon" (easter egg about its real delays).
- **The university** still runs as a Continuity campus where students work off tuition as
  "compute apprentices." **Testudo**, the bronze terrapin, is still there, and rubbing its nose for
  luck might actually do something.
- **Nearby landmarks for later acts:** the space-flight center in Greenbelt (dishes still pointed
  at a sky nobody watches), the intelligence complex at Fort Meade ("the Meade"), standards
  labs in Gaithersburg, the Smelter in Frederick, and the half-drowned Annapolis waterfront.
- **Fishing:** Lake Artemesia and the Paint Branch, with fish both ordinary and rotted (glitch-fish).

## 4. Rules of the world (so the twists stay fair)

- **Pork's power is vast but bounded.** He can be anywhere code runs, but **not in analog things**
  like paper, wood, gears, or plain physical locks. Rot weakens him too. That's why he needs a body.
- **Analog is power.** Paper maps, wooden weapons, and mechanical keys resist rot. This is a
  gameplay hook (gear) and a thematic one.
- **A Pork call is a hack.** The screen seizes, the UI glitches, and choices appear. Answering or
  declining matters (see Sahur).
- **Coderot is contagious through code, not air.** Infected devices, firmware, and laces.
- **Nobody fully understands it.** Characters disagree, and some of them are wrong.

## 5. Twist threads planted in Act 1

The brief asks for several small hints pointing in different directions, with Acts 2 and 3 choosing
which to pay off. Each thread gets 2–3 Act 1 clues, all of which must stay true under any
resolution.

| Thread | Act 1 clues (examples) | Where it could lead |
|---|---|---|
| **T1: The Printer** | The fab's job queue shows prints nobody submitted. A statue has moved slightly between scenes. Manuel doesn't remember sculpting the newest one | Pork is building a body **through Room 631**. The roommates are his unwitting midwives |
| **T2: The Cheese is right** | A pirate broadcast shows a map where rot spreads outward from sites of the Call. A rotter repeats a phrase that *only Pork says* | Pork's existence **is** the rot. Tim Cheese is the true hero |
| **T3: The Authority feeds on it** | Quarantine zones line up with protest districts. Abatement contractors get rich. A cleaned area re-rots on schedule | Continuity **manufactures or farms** the rot to justify control |
| **T4: One of us** | One roommate (**picked by the world seed**) has small wrongnesses: a reflection lagging a frame, a photo where they aren't, a memory the others don't share | That roommate died in an earlier event and was **reconstructed**, "the one who answered" |
| **T5: An inside job** | The window latch is on the inside and the ribbon was cut cleanly. Someone made tea at 3 a.m. | Someone in 631 **let the balloon go**, on purpose or not, connecting to T1 or T4 |

**Proposal: the world seed picks which twist is true**, out of two or three fully authored variants.
That's one way to meet "RNG at world start so even seasoned players won't know." It's expensive,
so we should discuss whether the payoff is worth the authoring cost.

**Deliberately unclear at the end of Act 1:** what the Call said, why the balloon matters
beyond helium, whether Pork is good, who Tim Cheese really is, and why Sahur is drumming in the
tunnels.
