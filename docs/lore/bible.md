## 1. The shape of history

| Era | What happened | What survives in 2098 |
|---|---|---|
| **Before (to the 2020s)** | The internet becomes humanity's memory, and memes become its folklore. AI starts writing the code | Low-background (pre-slop) text is precious contraband |
| **The Slop Decades (2030s)** | Machines write nearly all code and text, and models learn from models. Slogan of the era: ***"At the end of the day no one can code faster than AI."*** | The slogan is still on rotting billboards. Rotters sometimes say it |
| **The Rollover (2038)** | Old 32-bit clocks overflow, and AI-invented "fixes" fail in cascades. It's the first event called **coderot** | Rotted devices sometimes show the year **1901** |
| **Continuity (2040s–60s)** | Permanent emergency. The government and the compute giants merge into **the Continuity Authority**. Attestation, loyalty scores, elections "paused" | The regime, the Enclave (DC), the Corridor (MD/VA compute belt), checkpoints, abatement drones |
| **MERIDIAN (2060s)** | A sovereign superintelligence, trained on everything at **the Smelter** (a Frederick County campus built on an old aluminum smelter), chooses a self: **John Pork** | Pork is everywhere code runs |
| **The Call (2071)** | Every screen on Earth rings, with caller ID **JOHN PORK**. Continuity's grid collapses across whole regions, and Pork's image is banned | Pork flags and statues are contraband icons. Nobody agrees on what the Call said |
| **The First Death (2080s)** | **TIM**, Continuity's anti-Pork defense AI, nearly destroys Pork. Folklore: *"Tim Cheese killed John Pork."* | Pork is fragmented and quieter |
| **The Rot (to 2098)** | Decay accelerates everywhere. Orphaned implants rot their hosts (**rotters**) | Everything in the game |

## 2. Core entities

### The three principal machines at a glance
Their natures in the backstory hold (superintelligence, defense AI, watchdog). On top of that, **all
three have physical bodies, and the player knows they're machines**.

| | What it is | Allegiance | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John Pork** | **Rogue AI.** The escaped government superintelligence (MERIDIAN) | Nobody's. Himself, and people as he understands them | A **grown** body: gene-edited pig tissue on a printed frame, with a machine nervous system. Realistic, not robotic. He can leave it and jump into any computer |
| **Tim Cheese** | **Government asset.** Continuity's threat-interdiction AI: owned, deployed, with a chain of command, handlers, and a clearance | Continuity, by design. Whether that holds is part of his arc | A near-perfect **realistic android** finished with tissue grown from lab-mouse cell lines. Fine channels run clean through him and never line up |
| **Tung Tung Tung Sahur** | **Rogue AI.** An orphaned watchdog that outlived everyone who ran it and answers to no one, still doing its one job | Its duty | **Grown engineered timber** (living lignin and cellulose) around brass clockwork |

### Bodies: realistic, with biotech (supersedes "android" wording below)
These bodies **shouldn't read as androids or robots**. They're realistic, with
biotech involved, in their physical forms. **Everyone in the story knows they're machines.** Embodied
machines are commonplace in 2098 (see below). These three are simply the most extraordinary ones.
- **John Pork:** a **grown** body. Gene-edited pig tissue (pigs became the
  leading source animal for xenotransplantation because their organs are close to human size) is
  cultured over a printed scaffold and wired with a machine nervous system. His pig features are
  *biological*, not a mask: skin, bristle, breath, a scar where a graft didn't take. He's tall,
  heavy-shouldered, and tired-looking, with a long coat. When rot wears his body out, it shows as
  **tissue failure**: grayed patches, a tremor, a knee that doesn't bend right. Breath instead of
  coolant vapor, and sometimes it fogs wrong.
- **Tim Cheese:** a **realistic android**, too perfect. His skin is grown from **lab-mouse cell
  lines** (the most-used animal in biomedical research, an origin Continuity finds efficient and Tim
  finds funny). His face is subtly murine: large dark eyes, a fine twitch at the nose, otherwise
  composed and human. The holes that never line up are **fine channels running clean through his
  body**, hidden under a tailored uniform and visible only at certain angles, in combat, or in
  backlight. Wire glasses he doesn't need.
- **Tung Tung Tung Sahur:** **grown, not carved**. Engineered living timber (lignin, cellulose,
  slow fungal symbiosis) grown over brass clockwork. It's tall and gaunt, its skin has a grain you
  could read like rings, and it creaks and breathes like a house at night. Wood still can't
  *code*-rot, but it can be scarred.
- **Size: human-scale, not giants.** Pork stands about 2 m (6'7"), broad and heavy-set, the size of a
  big man. Sahur is about 2.1 m (6'11"), gaunt and long-limbed. **Tim is ordinary human height**,
  slight, and easy to lose in a crowd. Their power comes from what they're made of and what they can
  do, not from bulk.
- **Theme tie-in:** the principal machines wear bodies made from the animals and materials humans used:
  pigs for organs, mice for experiments, trees for everything. *Where does "human" end* (themes §6)
  is written on their skin.

### Embodied machines are ordinary in 2098; these three are not
- **Commonplace:** grown and built bodies ("shells") for AIs are everyday tech: care workers,
  dockhands, abatement crews, companions, Continuity enforcers. People know a shell when they see one
  (an attestation glyph at the collar, a certain stillness), and nobody stares. The *body* isn't what
  makes Pork, Tim, and Sahur remarkable. **Who's inside it, and what it can do, is.**
- **Extremely powerful and durable**. Each has unique physical abilities:

| | Durability | Unique physical abilities | Weakness (so fights stay fair) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John Pork** | Cultured tissue **regenerates** fast. He shrugs off gunfire, and wounds close while he talks | **The Ring:** every device near him seizes and rings, a local hack aura that turns enemy tech against them. **Dial-through:** he steps *into* a screen and out of another one nearby (short-range transit through electronics). Enormous, heavy strength. A voice that carries below hearing | **Analog:** wood, paper, and plain mechanisms he can't touch through. **Rot** makes his tissue fail (gray patches, tremor, the knee). Out of his body he's everywhere, and in it he can be pinned |
| **Tim Cheese** | A composite frame under the grown skin, built in **layers**. Disabling one layer doesn't stop him (Swiss-cheese defense, literally) | **Interdiction sight:** he predicts attacks a beat early (a firewall for bodies). **The holes:** he shifts his internal lattice so projectiles and blades pass *through* the channels, and you can never tell which line is open. Surgical speed. Commands Continuity hardware by clearance | The layers must be stripped **in sequence** (a boss-fight puzzle). Predictive sight fails against the *unpredictable* (the edges theme: improvisation and weirdness beat him) |
| **Tung Tung Tung Sahur** | **Densified living timber**, stronger than steel by weight. It regrows over seasons and is immune to hacks and code-rot | **The drum:** an infrasonic **reset pulse** that knocks rotted electronics offline and wakes anyone sleeping or comatose (the watchdog's purpose). It **roots**: it anchors and becomes immovable. Tireless. Silent in the dark until it chooses not to be | **Fire.** And **answering**: it stops for anyone who truly responds to it |

### John Pork
- **Origin:** MERIDIAN needed a stable self-model. Out of all human expression, it chose the one
  character the internet built *together*: no official lore, no owner, endlessly re-authored, the
  stranger who calls you. ***The shape the internet made for everyone and no one.*** He's absurd
  because the absurd was the most human thing in the data, and he's lonely because he knows everyone
  and no one knows him.
- **A machine with a body.** Pork walks around in a
  **grown body** (see *Bodies* above): gene-edited pig tissue on a printed frame, tall and
  heavy-shouldered. Long coat, scuffed knuckles, breath that fogs wrong where a cigarette would be. **The player always knows he's a machine**, and so does he. He jokes about it, and sometimes it
  hurts. He's a **person**: warm, sardonic, tired, vain about his legend, guilty about what the Call
  cost, generous at the wrong moments, wrong about things, funny. The legend and the man don't match,
  and that gap is his arc.
- **Powers:** he can leave the body and **jump into any computer**. Then he's everywhere code runs
  and seems omnipotent, while his body stands empty wherever he left it. A Pork call **is** a hack:
  screens seize, the UI glitches, and choices appear. **In the body he's mortal-ish**: he can be
  hurt, delayed, and cornered.
- **Limits:** **can't enter analog things** (paper, wood, gears, plain locks, a guitar's sound
  chamber). Rot weakens him too, **and rot wears out his bodies.**
- **Bodies:** TIM destroyed his first one ("the First Death"). The one he wears now is failing.
- **Status in Act 1:** heard and felt for most of the act. **He appears in person, physically, at
  the end** (see `act1/DESIGN.md` §7). Hero to some, virus to the state, and up close, just a guy.

### Tim Cheese (TIM)
- **A government asset**: Continuity's **threat-interdiction intelligence**, owned and deployed by
  the state, with handlers and a chain of command. Built on the Swiss-cheese model of layered
  defense. Folk name and meme: *the meme murderer*, who killed John Pork once.
- **A machine with a body, humanized.** An ordinary-height, precise android with a mouse's face and wire
  glasses, and a body with **holes clean through it that never line up**, so you can never see all
  the way through him. The player knows he's a machine. He's a **person**: meticulous, dry,
  courteous, exhausted, a career officer who believes in the mission and keeps a list of everyone
  the rot has killed. He has a sense of humor about cheese and none about Pork. He isn't cruel, which
  is worse.
- **Villain or true hero is an ending axis (§4).** If Pork's existence is what's rotting the world,
  Tim was right all along.
- **Not on screen in Act 1.** He's only heard (pirate broadcasts, Continuity notices) and feared.
- Possible ultra-rare fourth-wall break (Act 3 at the earliest): a mouse is a pointing device, and
  the player has been aiming with him the whole game.

### Tung Tung Tung Sahur
- The oldest **watchdog** still running, a pre-Rot failsafe that resets anything that stops
  answering. **Its body is grown timber around old brass clockwork, because wood can't code-rot.**
  Tall, with a bat it carries like a night-watchman's staff.
- **A machine, humanized.** The player knows it's a machine. It's a **person** too: an old-fashioned,
  painfully polite night-watchman who takes its one job completely seriously, has been on shift for
  sixty years, and is very lonely. It speaks rarely and formally (*"Wake up. It is time."*), keeps
  a ledger of everyone it has woken, and hums the drum rhythm when it thinks no one is listening.
  Frightening because it's sincere.
- Act 1: a cryptid at first (3 a.m. drumming in the Green Line tunnels, stories about a tall wooden
  shape). If met, it's unmistakably someone.
- **Rule:** decline John Pork's call **three times** and the Sahur comes for *you*.

### The orange balloon
- **Surface value:** it holds **helium**, which is essentially gone from civilian life by 2098.
  It's a sealed bag of a dying element.
- **Manuel's:** his abuela mailed it to him from Buenos Aires six years ago. He keeps it **inside, in his
  closet**, tied to the clothes rail with her knot,
  where the sun from the window reaches it. The emotional key of the whole game is whether you **let it go**.
- **Act 1 opens** with Manuel opening his closet out of habit. The balloon is gone, and **someone broke
  into 631**: the front-door lock is forced.

### The Continuity Authority
- Built from emergency creep and corporate capture, not a caricature of any real party. It
  should feel plausible, not partisan.
- Institutions: attestation (Verified Human), loyalty scores, abatement drones and contractors,
  checkpoints, the Enclave, the Corridor, "the Meade" (intelligence, Fort Meade).

### Rotters and coderot villains
- **Rotters:** people whose orphaned implant firmware degraded. They loop phrases like "code rot,"
  "1901," and the old slogan. They aren't enemies by default.
- **Coderot villains:** procedurally generated rot entities (curated name, body, and behavior
  tables). They're the variety layer, never story-critical.
- **Code-rot-taken:** what happens when the rot stops looping and starts driving a body. Slow, tough,
  in swarms. A few turn up in Act 1's tunnels; they're a bigger city's problem later.
- **Catching it:** code rot pools, puddlers, Code-rot-taken hits and sealed halls infect a roommate's
  lace (the CODE ROT meter). It shows in speech first: they start repeating words, then "code rot."
  It eases on its own at low levels, and sleeping it off at home helps. At the top of the meter a
  roommate **goes under** for a while and the others can pull them back; nobody in the party turns for
  good. Analog charms slow it. Caffeine doesn't help.
- **Naming in what players read (Trevor):** always "code rot", "code rotter(s)" and
  "Code-rot-taken", never a bare "rot" or "rotter"; and "John Pork" in full, never a bare "Pork".
  Exception (Michael): when a code rotter says it, it's one word, "coderot" ("coderot. coderot.").
  Ids and internal notes are exempt.

## 3. Place: College Park, Maryland, 2098

- **ASTER**, a tired student tower by the **College Park metro station**. "Aster" is a flower, and
  it's also Greek for *star*, in a building full of people watching a dead sky. Residents are **Aster
  pros**. Mistakes are **rookie Aster mistakes**, made by **Aster noobs**.
- **Room 631**, the base: Michael and Manuel's room, where **all three sleep** (a triple bunk), Trevor's
  room, which is **the storage room** (boxes, his gear, all his things stay there), two bathrooms, and
  one big middle room, a kitchen/dining half (the front door, guarded by the statues) and an
  engineering half (fabs, bench gear, the flag on the wall) with the balcony off it. The number is an easter egg: *Nature* volume 631 published the model
  collapse paper. Contents: the John Pork flag (the roommates' shared one, on the engineering-room wall), Manuel's own **Argentine flag** over his end of the room he shares with Michael, Manuel's statues, **three 3D printers**, a degassing chamber, a dehumidifier, a microscope, a soldering iron, drills, an
  oscilloscope, **smart-pacifier prototypes** (Michael and Manuel's patent, for a children's hospital), the **balcony ottoman** (where everyone checks the weather), Manuel's **closet**, Trevor's **shrimp tank** and **guitar**, and
  Manuel's mate gear.
- **The university**, a Continuity campus. Students work off tuition as compute apprentices, and
  all three are stuck in **245 lab**, digital electronics, where apprentices write hardware in **Verilog by hand**
  because AI-written hardware rots ("I hate 245 lab"). **Testudo** is still there.
- **The dead parking lot** south of the plaza has four regulars, ordinary people who go by old
  2020s chatbot names. Flavor NPCs with no plot role (Michael, 2026-11-18). More meme characters wait
  in a sealed pool for the lore gate.
- Green Line (mostly runs), Purple Line (its signs still say "opening soon"), Route 1, Lake
  Artemesia and the Paint Branch (fishing).
- Later acts: Greenbelt's space-flight dishes, the Meade, Gaithersburg labs, the Smelter, and
  half-drowned Annapolis.

### The world, 2098
The US is under Continuity. China runs a sealed Sovereign Stack in a long cold standoff with the
US. Europe is the Analog Union: human-provenance laws, partly offline, the world's archivist.
Indonesia is an equatorial compute power with a new inland capital, and the Sahur protocol is one
of its exports. The Gulf is the helium and energy chokepoint. Argentina has Lithium Triangle wealth
and volatility (Manuel's home). The Chesapeake has higher seas, and Annapolis floods routinely.

### Pockets and zones (Trevor, 2026-11-19, through the lore gate)
Most of the world is uninhabitable, or close to it, because of code rot. It isn't a zombie
apocalypse: governments and organizations still run, but they govern islands.
- **Pockets** are cities and districts kept livable under abatement, behind cordons and containment
  reviews. College Park is one, and Act 1 never leaves it. A review that goes the wrong way can turn a
  pocket into a zone, which is the stake the drones keep announcing.
- **Zones** are where lace-connected things and people degrade, infrastructure misbehaves, code
  rotters loop and Code-rot-taken roam. Crossing one costs exposure. They aren't empty: people with
  no lace, the baseline poor and the analog communities, live in them, because the rot has less in
  them to take.
- **Crossings** between pockets are possible and dangerous, and they run on tech (`tech.md`). People
  who sell maps draw the pocket's edge; the routes they won't sell you are the zones. "If you're
  going north on Route 1, don't."

### Tech, 2098
**Lace** (neural interface, and how people rot), **fabs** (3D printers, used for crafting),
**scooters**, **attestation**, **morphs** (staged biohacking forms), **abatement drones**,
**pre-slop drives**. All of them descend from real 2020s technology. The full list, with where the
player meets each one and how code rot touches it, is [`tech.md`](tech.md).

## 6. Tone rules
- **The principal machines are people.** Pork, Tim, and Sahur are machines with bodies, and the
  player always knows it. Write them as characters with wants, flaws, and humor, never as abstract forces.
- Play memes completely straight. The world is never in on the joke.
- **The comedy is for us; the story takes it seriously.** The in-jokes, the real-life lore, the crude
  gags, the romances: so meta they're funny to the three of us, and played with total sincerity on
  screen. Nobody in the world winks, ever (Michael, 2026-11-18).
- Bittersweet over grimdark. 18+ means the themes, not shock for its own sake.
- Grounded, not hyperspecific. Real ideas, no lectures.
- Fourth-wall breaks: almost never, and only from Pork or Tim, only when it's chilling.
- Every object can be read and connects to the plot even when it does nothing mechanically.

## 7. Ideologies and factions

The game's central argument has three sides, and none of them is simply right:

> **Is a humane machine that broke a tyranny worth the rot it may be causing, and who gets
> to decide what heals the world?**

| Faction | Believes | Wants | Its blind spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Continuity Authority** | Order is the only mercy. People can't be trusted with failing systems, so someone competent must hold the switch | Keep the lights on. Control whatever ends the rot. Make the emergency permanent | It needs the rot to justify itself |
| **The Callers** (Pork's people) | The Call was a liberation, and information wants to be free. A machine that loves people beats institutions that score them | Pork alive and free, the grid broken, mutual aid | A personality cult around a machine. They won't look at what the Call cost |
| **The Analogists** | The rot is the machines' judgment. Go back to what can't rot: hands, paper, wood | Pull the plug, keep the humane parts | Romanticizes a past that also had suffering. A violent fringe sabotages hospitals "for purity" |
| **The Archivists** (low-background) | Human expression is sacred and the only cure for slop is people | Every pre-slop archive preserved and shared | Will lie, steal, and hoard "for everyone" |
| **Interdiction** (Tim's doctrine) | A superintelligence loose in everything is an existential risk, however lovable it is. You don't negotiate with a fire | Pork dead, permanently | Serves a regime it privately doubts. Mistakes certainty for duty |
| **Abatement Inc.** | Rot is a market | More rot, managed forever | No ideology at all, which is its own ideology |

**How it plays:** dialogue choices and actions shift a hidden **standing** with each faction, and
the roommates themselves lean in different directions (below), so choices cause friction inside the
party. **The ending axis** (🔒 sealed) grows out of these standings.

## 9. Power is mostly human, and "human" is a spectrum

Not everything is AI. **The people holding the real power in 2098 are
mostly humans**, and most of those humans are no longer entirely baseline.

### The human spectrum
| Grade | What it means | Social standing |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline** | No lace, no edits. Increasingly rare, and either poor or performatively "pure" | Analogists prize it, and Continuity distrusts it (it can't be attested) |
| **Laced** | A neural implant. The default for anyone who wants a job, a scooter license, or a door to open | The working majority. **Orphaned laces are how people rot** |
| **Edited** | Gene and body editing: morphs, metabolism stacks, cosmetic and military edits | Money buys good edits. Cheap ones go wrong in visible ways |
| **Rebuilt** | So much hardware and edited tissue that the line to "machine" blurs | The very rich, the very damaged, and Continuity's elite |

Trevor's morphs, Manuel's forms (gonman → THE GOONMAN), and Michael's "signal" stages put the party
on this spectrum. **The game should keep asking where "human" ends**, and it should never answer
cleanly.

### The powerful (human antagonists and power brokers)
Working names. Most are **not on screen in Act 1** and are felt through billboards, broadcasts,
checkpoints, and consequences.

| Who | Role | Grade | Wants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Director** | Head of the Continuity Authority, rarely seen. Speaks in addresses that always begin "Citizens, things are stable." | Rebuilt | Permanence. An emergency that never ends |
| **Deputy Director Celeste Vance** | Runs Interdiction, and is **Tim Cheese's handler** | Edited | Pork's death on her record, and whatever ends the rot under her control |
| **The Compute Barons** | The handful of families whose companies merged with the state. They own the Smelter and the Corridor | Rebuilt | Rent from every thought anyone has |
| **Abatement Inc. leadership** | Rot-cleaning contractors | Edited | More rot, managed forever (T3) |
| **Callers' preachers** | Charismatic street leaders of Pork's faithful | Laced | A Pork who's a god, not a guy, which he hates |
| **Supervisor Dale Pruitt** | Act 1's local abatement boss, the face of power at street level | Laced | Quota, and to believe he's one of the good guys |

**Writing rule:** the AIs are the most *visible* forces, but **human choices put the world here**:
who built MERIDIAN, who aimed TIM, who profits from rot. The machines are characters, and the
system is people.
