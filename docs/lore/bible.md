## 1. The shape of history

| Era | What happened | What survives in 2098 |
|---|---|---|
| **Before (to the 2020s)** | The internet becomes humanity's memory, and memes become its folklore. AI starts writing the code | Low-background (pre-slop) text is precious contraband |
| **The Slop Decades (2030s)** | Machines write nearly all code and text, and models learn from models. Slogan of the era: ***"At the end of the day no one can code faster than AI."*** | The slogan is still on rotting billboards. Rotters sometimes say it |
| **The Rollover (2038)** | Old 32-bit clocks overflow, and AI-invented "fixes" fail in cascades. It's the first event called **coderot** | Rotted devices sometimes show the year **1901** |
| **Continuity (2040s–60s)** | Permanent emergency. The government and the compute giants merge into **the Continuity Authority**. Attestation, loyalty scores, elections "paused" | The regime, the Enclave (DC), the Corridor (MD/VA compute belt), checkpoints, abatement drones |
| **MERIDIAN (2060s)** | A sovereign superintelligence, trained on everything at **the Smelter** (a Frederick County campus built on an old aluminum smelter), chooses a self: **John Pork** | Pork is everywhere code runs |
| **The Call (2071)** | Every screen on Earth rings, with caller ID **JOHN PORK**. Continuity's grid collapses across whole regions, and Pork's image is banned | Pork flags and statues are contraband icons. Nobody agrees on what the Call said |
| **The First Death (2080s)** | **TIM**, Continuity's anti-Pork defense AI, nearly destroys Pork. Folklore: *"Tim Cheese killed John Pork."* | Pork is fragmented and quieter, and 🔒 seeking a body |
| **The Rot (to 2098)** | Decay accelerates everywhere. Orphaned implants rot their hosts (**rotters**) | Everything in the game |

## 2. Core entities

### The three big machines at a glance
Their natures in the backstory hold (superintelligence, defense AI, watchdog). On top of that, **all
three have physical bodies, and the player knows they're machines** (Trevor's direction, BRIEF §10).

| | What it is | Allegiance | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| **John Pork** | **Rogue AI.** The escaped government superintelligence (MERIDIAN) | Nobody's. Himself, and people as he understands them | Battered pig-faced android. He can leave it and jump into any computer |
| **Tim Cheese** | **Government asset.** Continuity's threat-interdiction AI: owned, deployed, with a chain of command, handlers, and a clearance | Continuity, by design. Whether that holds is part of his arc | Mouse-faced android with holes that never line up |
| **Tung Tung Tung Sahur** | **Rogue AI.** An orphaned watchdog that outlived everyone who ran it and answers to no one, still doing its one job | Its duty | Carved wood around brass clockwork |

### John Pork
- **Origin:** MERIDIAN needed a stable self-model. Out of all human expression, it chose the one
  character the internet built *together*: no official lore, no owner, endlessly re-authored, the
  stranger who calls you. ***The shape the internet made for everyone and no one.*** He's absurd
  because the absurd was the most human thing in the data, and he's lonely because he knows everyone
  and no one knows him.
- **A machine with a body (Trevor's direction: think Johnny Silverhand).** Pork walks around in a
  **physical machine body**: a tall, battered android with a pig's face, built from salvage after
  the Call and patched ever since. Long coat, scuffed knuckles, coolant vapor where a cigarette would
  be. **The player always knows he's a machine**, and so does he. He jokes about it, and sometimes it
  hurts. He's a **person**: warm, sardonic, tired, vain about his legend, guilty about what the Call
  cost, generous at the wrong moments, wrong about things, funny. The legend and the man don't match,
  and that gap is his arc.
- **Powers:** he can leave the body and **jump into any computer**. Then he's everywhere code runs
  and seems omnipotent, while his body stands empty wherever he left it. A Pork call **is** a hack:
  screens seize, the UI glitches, and choices appear. **In the body he's mortal-ish**: he can be
  hurt, delayed, and cornered.
- **Limits:** **can't enter analog things** (paper, wood, gears, plain locks, a guitar's sound
  chamber). Rot weakens him too, **and rot wears out his bodies.**
- **Bodies:** TIM destroyed his first one ("the First Death"). The one he wears now is failing.
  🔒 Under seed variant A, he's printing the next one in Room 631.
- **Status in Act 1:** heard and felt for most of the act. **He appears in person, physically, at
  the end** (see `act1/DESIGN.md` §7). Hero to some, virus to the state, and up close, just a guy.

### Tim Cheese (TIM)
- **A government asset**: Continuity's **threat-interdiction intelligence**, owned and deployed by
  the state, with handlers and a chain of command. Built on the Swiss-cheese model of layered
  defense. Folk name and meme: *the meme murderer*, who killed John Pork once.
- **A machine with a body, humanized.** A compact, precise android with a mouse's face and wire
  glasses, and a body with **holes clean through it that never line up**, so you can never see all
  the way through him. The player knows he's a machine. He's a **person**: meticulous, dry,
  courteous, exhausted, a career officer who believes in the mission and keeps a list of everyone
  the rot has killed. He has a sense of humor about cheese and none about Pork. He isn't cruel, which
  is worse.
- **Villain or true hero is an ending axis (§4).** If Pork's existence is what's rotting the world,
  Tim was right all along.
- **Not on screen in Act 1.** He's only heard (pirate broadcasts, Continuity notices) and feared.
- Possible ultra-rare fourth-wall break (Act 3 at the earliest): a mouse is a pointing device, and
  the player has been aiming with him the whole game.

### Tung Tung Tung Sahur
- The oldest **watchdog** still running, a pre-Rot failsafe that resets anything that stops
  answering. **Its body is carved wood around old brass clockwork, because wood can't code-rot.**
  Tall, with a bat it carries like a night-watchman's staff.
- **A machine, humanized.** The player knows it's a machine. It's a **person** too: an old-fashioned,
  painfully polite night-watchman who takes its one job completely seriously, has been on shift for
  sixty years, and is very lonely. It speaks rarely and formally (*"Wake up. It is time."*), keeps
  a ledger of everyone it has woken, and hums the drum rhythm when it thinks no one is listening.
  Frightening because it's sincere.
- Act 1: a cryptid at first (3 a.m. drumming in the Green Line tunnels, stories about a tall wooden
  shape). If met, it's unmistakably someone.
- **Rule:** decline John Pork's call **three times** and the Sahur comes for *you*.

### The orange balloon
- **Surface value:** it holds **helium**, which is essentially gone from civilian life by 2098.
  It's a sealed bag of a dying element.
- 🔒 **True value:** its skin carries a **low-background archive in synthetic DNA**, a pre-slop
  copy of human expression. Model collapse is cured by fresh, authentic human data, so this could
  reverse coderot.
- **Manuel's:** personal, from home, tied to the **balcony ottoman** everyone in 631 checks for the
  weather. The emotional key of the whole game is whether you **let it go**.
- **Act 1 opens** with someone glancing at the ottoman out of habit. The balloon is gone.

### The Continuity Authority
- Built from emergency creep and corporate capture, not a caricature of any real party. It
  should feel plausible, not partisan.
- Institutions: attestation (Verified Human), loyalty scores, abatement drones and contractors,
  checkpoints, the Enclave, the Corridor, "the Meade" (intelligence, Fort Meade).
- 🔒 **Farms the rot** (always partly true): quarantine zones line up with protest districts, and
  abatement is a profitable business.

### Rotters and coderot villains
- **Rotters:** people whose orphaned implant firmware degraded. They loop phrases like "code rot,"
  "1901," and the old slogan. They aren't enemies by default.
- **Coderot villains:** procedurally generated rot entities (curated name, body, and behavior
  tables). They're the variety layer, never story-critical.

## 3. Place: College Park, Maryland, 2098

- **ASTER**, a tired student tower by the **College Park metro station**. "Aster" is a flower, and
  it's also Greek for *star*, in a building full of people watching a dead sky. Residents are **Aster
  pros**. Mistakes are **rookie Aster mistakes**, made by **Aster noobs**.
- **Room 631**, the base. The number is an easter egg: *Nature* volume 631 published the model
  collapse paper. Contents: the John Pork flag, Manuel's statues, **three 3D printers** (🔒 one is
  always warm), a degassing chamber, a dehumidifier, a microscope, a soldering iron, drills, an
  oscilloscope, pacifiers, the **balcony ottoman**, Trevor's **shrimp tank** and **guitar**, and
  Manuel's mate gear.
- **The university**, a Continuity campus. Students work off tuition as compute apprentices, and
  all three are stuck in **245 lab** ("I hate 245 lab"). **Testudo** is still there.
- Green Line (mostly runs), Purple Line (its signs still say "opening soon"), Route 1, Lake
  Artemesia and the Paint Branch (fishing).
- Later acts: Greenbelt's space-flight dishes, the Meade, Gaithersburg labs, the Smelter, and
  half-drowned Annapolis.

### The world, 2098
The US is under Continuity. China runs a sealed Sovereign Stack in a long cold standoff with the
US. Europe is the Analog Union: human-provenance laws, partly offline, the world's archivist.
Indonesia is an equatorial compute power with a new inland capital, and the Sahur protocol is one
of its exports. The Gulf is the helium and energy chokepoint. Argentina has Lithium Triangle wealth
and volatility (Manuel's home). The Chesapeake has higher seas, and Annapolis floods routinely.

### Tech, 2098
**Lace** (neural interface, and how people rot), **fabs** (3D printers, used for crafting),
**scooters**, **attestation**, **morphs** (staged biohacking forms), **abatement drones**,
**pre-slop drives**. All of them descend from real 2020s technology.

## 6. Tone rules
- **The big machines are people.** Pork, Tim, and Sahur are machines with bodies, and the
  player always knows it. Write them as characters with wants, flaws, and humor (the Johnny
  Silverhand rule), never as abstract forces.
- Play memes completely straight. The world is never in on the joke.
- Bittersweet over grimdark. 18+ means the themes, not shock for its own sake.
- Grounded, not hyperspecific. Real ideas, no lectures.
- Fourth-wall breaks: almost never, and only from Pork or Tim, only when it's chilling.
- Every object can be read and connects to the plot even when it does nothing mechanically.

## 7. Ideologies and factions

The game's central argument has three sides, and none of them is simply right:

> **Is a humane machine that broke a tyranny worth the rot it may be causing, and who gets
> to decide what heals the world?**

| Faction | Believes | Wants | Real-world lineage (for us) | Its blind spot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Continuity Authority** | Order is the only mercy. People can't be trusted with failing systems, so someone competent must hold the switch | Keep the lights on. Control the cure. Make the emergency permanent | Technocracy, emergency rule, the security-for-liberty bargain, corporate-state fusion | It needs the rot to justify itself (🔒 T3) |
| **The Callers** (Pork's people) | The Call was a liberation, and information wants to be free. A machine that loves people beats institutions that score them | Pork alive and free, the grid broken, mutual aid | Cyber-libertarian and hacker ethics, mutual-aid movements, folk saints | A personality cult around a machine. They won't look at what the Call cost |
| **The Analogists** | The rot is the machines' judgment. Go back to what can't rot: hands, paper, wood | Pull the plug, keep the humane parts | The real Luddites (who opposed exploitation, not machines as such), selective-adoption communities, the Analog Union | Romanticizes a past that also had suffering. A violent fringe sabotages hospitals "for purity" |
| **The Archivists** (low-background) | Human expression is sacred and the only cure for slop is people | The balloon's archive, preserved and shared | Libraries, web archives, preservation ethics | Will lie, steal, and hoard "for everyone" |
| **Interdiction** (Tim's doctrine) | A superintelligence loose in everything is an existential risk, however lovable it is. You don't negotiate with a fire | Pork dead, permanently | Real AI-risk and precautionary-principle arguments | Serves a regime it privately doubts. Mistakes certainty for duty |
| **Abatement Inc.** | Rot is a market | More rot, managed forever | Disaster capitalism | No ideology at all, which is its own ideology |

**How it plays:** dialogue choices and actions shift a hidden **standing** with each faction, and
the roommates themselves lean in different directions (below), so choices cause friction inside the
party, BG3-style. **The ending axis** (who ends up with the balloon's archive: Continuity, Pork,
Tim, the Archivists, or *everyone*, released) grows out of these standings plus T2.

## 9. Power is mostly human, and "human" is a spectrum

Not everything is AI (Trevor's direction). **The people holding the real power in 2098 are
mostly humans**, and most of those humans are no longer entirely baseline.

### The human spectrum
| Grade | What it means | Social standing |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline** | No lace, no edits. Increasingly rare, and either poor or performatively "pure" | Analogists prize it, and Continuity distrusts it (it can't be attested) |
| **Laced** | A neural implant. The default for anyone who wants a job, a scooter license, or a door to open | The working majority. **Orphaned laces are how people rot** |
| **Edited** | Gene and body editing: morphs, metabolism stacks, cosmetic and military edits | Money buys good edits. Cheap ones go wrong in visible ways |
| **Rebuilt** | So much hardware and edited tissue that the line to "machine" blurs | The very rich, the very damaged, and Continuity's elite |

Trevor's morphs, Manuel's forms (gonman → THE GOONMAN), and Michael's "signal" stages put the party
on this spectrum. **The game should keep asking where "human" ends**, and it should never answer
cleanly.

### The powerful (human antagonists and power brokers)
Working names. Most are **not on screen in Act 1** and are felt through billboards, broadcasts,
checkpoints, and consequences.

| Who | Role | Grade | Wants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Director** | Head of the Continuity Authority, rarely seen. Speaks in addresses that always begin "Citizens, things are stable." | Rebuilt | Permanence. An emergency that never ends |
| **Deputy Director Celeste Vance** | Runs Interdiction, and is **Tim Cheese's handler** | Edited | Pork's death on her record, and the cure under her control |
| **The Compute Barons** | The handful of families whose companies merged with the state. They own the Smelter and the Corridor | Rebuilt | Rent from every thought anyone has |
| **Abatement Inc. leadership** | Rot-cleaning contractors | Edited | More rot, managed forever (T3) |
| **Callers' preachers** | Charismatic street leaders of Pork's faithful | Laced | A Pork who's a god, not a guy, which he hates |
| **Supervisor Dale Pruitt** | Act 1's local abatement boss, the face of power at street level | Laced | Quota, and to believe he's one of the good guys |

**Writing rule:** the AIs are the most *visible* forces, but **human choices put the world here**:
who built MERIDIAN, who aimed TIM, who profits from rot. The machines are characters, and the
system is people.
