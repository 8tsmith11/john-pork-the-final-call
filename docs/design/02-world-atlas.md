# 02. World Atlas

> **Status: draft v0, for review.** Spoiler-free. Where a place hides something that matters to
> the plot, this file says only that it does. The reveals live in `../sealed/`.
>
> Every region here is a real part of Maryland, Washington or northern Virginia, seventy years on.
> The map is small on purpose: the whole game fits inside a ninety-minute drive in 2026. In 2098
> nobody drives it in ninety minutes.

## How to read an entry

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Act** | When the party can first get there. Regions stay open afterwards unless the story closes them |
| **Look** | What the player sees first, and the color notes for art (see doc 10) |
| **Who holds it** | Faction control and who is quietly present |
| **Rot** | How decay shows up here specifically |
| **Getting around** | Travel inside the region and the hidden shortcut it hides |
| **People** | The kinds of NPC found here (named characters are in doc 04) |
| **To find** | Readable objects and books. Every one links to something else in the game |
| **Sound** | The music and the ambient bed |

## The shape of the map

Three rings, and the party crosses one per act.

1. **The neighborhood** (Act 1): College Park. On foot and by scooter.
2. **The county and the bay** (Act 2): north along Route 1 and the rail line to Baltimore, east to
   Annapolis and the water. By scooter, rail, and boat.
3. **The Corridor and the Enclave** (Act 3): west to Frederick and south into Washington. By
   permission, forgery, or force.

Two lines run through all three. **Route 1** is the old road, potholed and human, lined with
things that still work because nobody updated them. **The Beltway** is the wall: Continuity turned
the ring road into the Enclave's outer checkpoint line, and which side of it you live on is the
first thing anyone asks.

## Ring one: College Park

### 1. ASTER

- **Act:** 1. The home base for the whole game.
- **Look:** a twelve-storey student tower from the 2030s, concrete, with a lobby that was
  renovated once and regrets it. Warm windows in a cold facade. Room 631 is the warmest room in
  the game: wood floor, lamp light, three printers, bench gear, the flag, the busts by the door.
- **Who holds it:** nobody. A building super on Abatement's payroll, and tenants who look after
  each other. Residents call themselves Aster pros.
- **Rot:** small and domestic. Hallway clocks stuck at 1901. An elevator that announces floors
  that don't exist. A lobby camera that records a different night from the one that happened.
- **Getting around:** stairs, the elevator (unreliable in a way that can be learned), the roof.
  **Shortcut:** zipline cables from the roof antenna to the metro garage.
- **People:** students, a night-shift care worker in a grown body, a retired transit engineer, a
  family that has lived on the third floor since before the Call, the super.
- **To find:** the building directory with letters missing (the missing letters spell something);
  a lost-and-found box that is never emptied; the 245 lab textbook everyone owns and nobody has
  finished; a tenant newsletter that stopped in 2071 and resumed the next month without comment.
- **Sound:** a quiet late-night piece for 631 (see doc 10 for the brief). Pipes, a neighbor's
  television through the wall, the dehumidifier.

### 2. The metro plaza and the Green Line

- **Act:** 1.
- **Look:** a concrete plaza under sodium light, a shuttered row of stalls under the stairs, a
  billboard whose bottom third has rotted into the wrong advertisement. Below it, the station:
  tile, pillars, a platform clock at 1901. Below that, the maintenance tunnels: no warm colors at
  all until something wooden walks through them.
- **Who holds it:** Abatement runs the vans and the cordons. The stalls belong to people who were
  here first. The tunnels belong to whatever is down there.
- **Rot:** this is where the Act 1 outbreak starts. Signage lies. Ticket gates open for the wrong
  people. Rotters gather here because the station is warm.
- **Getting around:** on foot. Scooters on the plaza only. **Shortcuts:** the storm grate; the
  rail-grind along the unused Purple Line track.
- **People:** rotters, commuters who don't look up, a fence in a phone-repair stall, abatement
  crews on quota, a busker with a wooden instrument that is never out of tune.
- **To find:** the Purple Line sign that has said "opening soon" for seventy years; graffiti on one
  wall that makes three demands that can't all be true; a transit map with one station scratched
  off; a stall of dead phones, one of which still rings.
- **Sound:** a slow city-at-night piece on the plaza. In the tunnels the music drops out and the
  bed is water, distant ventilation, and, after three in the morning, drumming.

### 3. The campus

- **Act:** 1 (edge), 2 (interior).
- **Look:** brick and white columns, kept up for the brochure. A long lawn. A bronze terrapin
  with a nose polished gold by hands. Behind the brick, compute halls: windowless, humming, cold
  light under the doors.
- **Who holds it:** Continuity, politely. The university is a campus of the Authority, and students
  work off tuition as compute apprentices. 245 lab is here.
- **Rot:** institutional. Grades that change. A lecture that has been playing to an empty hall
  since the lecturer died. A library catalogue that returns books that were never written.
- **Getting around:** scooter on the paths. **Shortcut:** the steam tunnels under the lawn, which
  the apprentices use to skip attestation gates.
- **People:** apprentices, a registrar who can fix anything for a favor, a librarian with a locked
  room, a recruiter for the Authority who is good at his job.
- **To find:** the library's locked room of paper; an exam nobody passes, pinned up as a dare; the
  terrapin (rub the nose); a plaque to a department that the directory says never existed.
- **Sound:** a bright, slightly wrong piece, like a school song remembered badly. Sprinklers, a
  bell tower that strikes thirteen.

### 4. Route 1 and the airfield

- **Act:** 1.
- **Look:** a four-lane road gone to two, shopfronts with hand-painted signs over dead screens, a
  scooter repair yard, and at the east end a small grass airfield with a hangar that has been open
  since the first years of flight. It still has a windsock.
- **Who holds it:** shopkeepers. Analogists use the airfield hangar as a meeting hall, because
  nothing in it has a chip.
- **Rot:** light. This is the least rotted place in Act 1 and the player should feel it: prices on
  chalkboards, locks with keys, a cash drawer.
- **Getting around:** scooter. This is where scooter upgrades are bought.
- **People:** a mechanic, a paper-map seller, a diner cook who remembers every order, Analogist
  families, kids who have never been laced.
- **To find:** paper maps (they reveal fast-travel routes when read); a flight log with an entry
  on the night of the Call; a jar of keys with no doors; a menu with one item crossed out by hand
  every day and rewritten.
- **Sound:** warm and acoustic. Wood and wire only. A screen door, a bell on a shop door, wind
  across the field.

### 5. Lake Artemesia and the Paint Branch

- **Act:** 1.
- **Look:** a man-made lake with a footbridge and a small island, and the creek that feeds it
  running back through trees toward campus. Mist in the morning. The only place in Act 1 with a
  horizon.
- **Who holds it:** nobody. People fish.
- **Rot:** in the water. Ordinary fish and glitch-fish. Something on the island lights up at night.
- **Getting around:** the trail by scooter or on foot. A rowboat, once repaired.
- **People:** an old woman who fishes every dawn and has opinions about bait, a couple who meet on
  the bridge, a Continuity surveyor who is not here to fish.
- **To find:** the fishing log on the bridge, signed by strangers for fifty years; things on the
  hook that aren't fish (an old phone that still rings); a bench plaque with a date that hasn't
  happened yet.
- **Sound:** the quietest music in the game. Reeds, water, a single line of melody. Frogs.

## Ring two: the county and the bay

### 6. Greenbelt and the dishes

- **Act:** 2.
- **Look:** a planned town of curved streets and co-op shopfronts, built in the 1930s to prove a
  point about living together and still arguing about it. On the hill above it, a space-flight
  center: white dishes the size of houses, pointed at a sky nobody is paid to watch.
- **Who holds it:** the town runs itself by committee. Archivists keep the center, quietly, because
  its machines were built before the Slop Decades and its tapes are clean.
- **Rot:** the dishes still receive. What they receive is the question.
- **People:** committee members who can't agree on anything except each other, an Archivist cell,
  a technician who never left his post.
- **To find:** telemetry tapes; the co-op's minute books, a century of them; a clean room with one
  set of footprints.
- **Sound:** slow, wide, a little hopeful. Long tones. The dishes turning.

### 7. Beltsville: the farms

- **Act:** 2.
- **Look:** flat research farmland, long low barns, white fences. Agricultural science was done
  here for two hundred years. The newest barns are sealed and have airlocks.
- **Who holds it:** a Compute Baron's biotech subsidiary, on a Continuity lease. This is where
  grown bodies come from: care-worker shells, dockhand shells, enforcers.
- **Rot:** biological. Rot here means tissue that doesn't take, shells that wake up wrong, a field
  of them standing in the rain.
- **People:** tissue technicians, a veterinarian who took the wrong job for the right reasons,
  shells awaiting assignment who are, by any test the party can run, people.
- **To find:** breeding ledgers that go back before the Call; a barn the map doesn't show; a
  growth tank with a name written on it in marker.
- **Sound:** pastoral, then not. A music-box figure over low strings. Rain on sheet metal.

### 8. The refuge

- **Act:** 2.
- **Look:** thousands of acres of forest and wetland between the farms and the fort, fenced a
  century ago for wildlife and never built on. No signal. Boardwalks, blinds, a fire tower.
- **Who holds it:** the Analogists, in strength. Their largest settlement is here, built of timber,
  lit by lamps. So is their violent fringe, a day's walk deeper in.
- **Rot:** none, and that is its own unease. The party's gear stops helping them.
- **People:** settlers, a blacksmith, a schoolteacher with a slate, a hard man who thinks hospitals
  are the disease.
- **To find:** a library of hand-copied books with the copying errors lovingly preserved; a
  graveyard of smashed implants; tree rings counted and labeled with events.
- **Sound:** unaccompanied voices, hand percussion. Wind, birds, an axe.

### 9. The Meade

- **Act:** 2 (perimeter), 3 (inside).
- **Look:** a fort the size of a town. Black glass cubes behind berms, a marsh along the east
  fence, antenna farms, a parking lot with ten thousand spaces and forty cars.
- **Who holds it:** Interdiction. This is Deputy Director Vance's house, and Tim Cheese's.
- **Rot:** forbidden. The cleanest systems in Maryland, kept that way by people who do nothing else.
- **People:** officers, analysts who have not been home in years, a quartermaster who can be
  reasoned with, a list on a wall.
- **To find:** the wall; intercepts of calls nobody should have been able to record; a cafeteria
  menu that is the only thing here with a sense of humor.
- **Sound:** a clock-like pulse, strings held very still. Ventilation. Footsteps in step.

### 10. Baltimore: the port

- **Act:** 2.
- **Look:** a harbor city that kept working. Cranes, rowhouses with marble steps, a sugar
  refinery sign still lit red over the water, container stacks with whole neighborhoods living in
  them. The first real crowd the party has seen.
- **Who holds it:** contested. Continuity holds the port authority. The Callers hold the streets;
  this is their biggest congregation. Dockhand shells have a union.
- **Rot:** an outbreak in progress when the party arrives. Bigger than College Park's, and nobody
  is containing it.
- **People:** a Caller preacher who is very good and partly right, union stewards, smugglers,
  a doctor running a clinic out of a rowhouse, more rotters than anyone can help.
- **To find:** the Callers' book of what people say they heard; shipping manifests for helium;
  murals of Pork that get him slightly wrong in ways he would mind.
- **Sound:** the busiest music in the game: brass, drums, a choir that comes in late. Gulls,
  cranes, a crowd.

### 11. Annapolis and the bay

- **Act:** 2.
- **Look:** a colonial capital with the tide in its streets twice a day. A wooden statehouse dome
  above the waterline, an academy chapel, sailboats moored to second-floor windows. East of it,
  the bay: a long bridge with a missing span, and islands that are on old charts and not on the
  water.
- **Who holds it:** watermen, and a naval garrison that has stopped taking calls from Washington.
- **Rot:** in the tide tables. The water comes when the almanac says it won't.
- **Getting around:** boat. This is where the party gets one.
- **People:** crabbers, a harbor pilot, midshipmen with no navy, an island family that refused to
  leave and is now an hour offshore.
- **To find:** paper charts (the only accurate ones); a tide almanac annotated by four generations;
  a church bell on an island with no church.
- **Sound:** a slow waltz for accordion and bowed strings. Halyards, hulls, the bell.

## Ring three: the Corridor and the Enclave

### 12. The Corridor

- **Act:** 3.
- **Look:** the data-center belt, running from the Maryland suburbs across the river into northern
  Virginia. Windowless halls to the horizon, each the size of an airport terminal, cooling towers
  breathing steam, substations, and between them the towns that used to be here, kept as worker
  housing. Light pollution so heavy the night is brown.
- **Who holds it:** the Compute Barons, with Continuity as landlord and customer.
- **Rot:** industrial. Whole halls written off and sealed, still drawing power, still running
  something.
- **People:** hall technicians, water-rights lawyers, a town that sold its river, a night-shift
  worker who talks to the machines and gets answers.
- **To find:** a sealed hall; water meters; an employee handbook from the Slop Decades with the
  slogan on its cover.
- **Sound:** a low electrical hum that the music is tuned to. Fans. Rain that evaporates before it
  lands.

### 13. Gaithersburg: the standards

- **Act:** 3.
- **Look:** a low campus of labs behind a deer fence. This is where the country kept its official
  measures: the second, the kilogram, the volt.
- **Who holds it:** a handful of metrologists, protected by everyone, because every faction needs
  to know what time it is.
- **Rot:** the clocks. Somewhere on this campus is the reason so many devices think it is 1901.
- **People:** timekeepers, a physicist who has not slept properly in years, a Continuity liaison
  who is afraid of them.
- **To find:** the master clock; a logbook of leap seconds; a room of reference weights with one
  empty cradle.
- **Sound:** a piece built on a tick that slowly drifts out of time with itself.

### 14. Frederick and the Smelter

- **Act:** 3.
- **Look:** a brick market town with church spires, and beyond it in the valley a compute campus
  built on the footprint of an aluminum smelter: potlines turned server halls, a switchyard like a
  steel forest, mountains behind. The Catoctin ridge above it has a bunker nobody admits to.
- **Who holds it:** the Director. This is the seat of the thing.
- **Rot:** this is where it is deepest and where it is studied. Also, on the edge of town, an old
  army medical research post where things were grown long before bodies were.
- **People:** the town, which is ordinary and frightened; campus staff; a very old woman who used
  to work there.
- **To find:** the room where a team once laughed at a dashboard; the potline numbering, which
  still follows the smelter's; a guest book.
- **Sound:** the main theme, finally complete. Wind in the switchyard.

### 15. The Enclave

- **Act:** 3.
- **Look:** Washington inside the Beltway wall. Marble, lawns, clean screens, cherry trees that
  flower on schedule. Everything works. Attestation gates every two blocks. A long green mall with
  monuments at each end and nobody on the grass.
- **Who holds it:** Continuity, entirely.
- **Rot:** officially none. In practice it is managed out of sight: there are neighborhoods the
  map shows as parks.
- **People:** the attested, who are polite and afraid of losing it; functionaries; embassy staff
  from the Analog Union and the Sovereign Stack, watching each other; cleaners in grown bodies.
- **To find:** the national archive's original documents, on parchment, which nothing can rot; a
  metro system that runs on time and announces stations in a single calm voice; the Director's
  addresses, all of them, and what changes between versions.
- **Sound:** stately, major key, and hollow. One identical voice on every speaker.

## Places heard about and not visited

They arrive as news tickers, radio, embassy staff, postcards and cargo. They are not maps.

| Place | How it reaches the party |
|---|---|
| **Argentina** | Manuel's postcards, his mate, lithium convoys, a call home that keeps not connecting |
| **The Analog Union** | Paper mail, embassy staff in the Enclave, archivists who trained there |
| **The Sovereign Stack** | Rumor, sealed cargo at Baltimore, a broadcast nobody can decode |
| **Indonesia** | The Sahur's make and its manners, port workers' stories, a drum pattern |
| **The Gulf** | Helium prices, convoy news, the reason a party balloon is worth a car |
| **Norfolk** | The other outbreak on the news; the fleet that isn't answering |

## Rules for every region

1. **One thing that still works.** Each region has something analog and reliable that the player
   learns to trust: a paper map, a tide almanac, a key, a bell.
2. **One thing that lies.** Each region has a system that reports the wrong thing, and learning
   how it lies is a puzzle.
3. **Readable objects connect.** Nothing readable is filler. Each one names a person in doc 04, a
   place in this file, or an event in the history. A player who reads everything should be able
   to draw the web.
4. **The roommates have opinions.** Each region has at least three lines per roommate on arrival,
   and one of the three is at home there in a way the others aren't.
5. **A fishing spot**, a **scooter trick**, and a **hidden route** in every outdoor region.
6. **Rot looks different everywhere.** Domestic in ASTER, institutional on campus, biological at
   the farms, absent in the refuge, forbidden at the Meade, managed in the Enclave.

## Open questions for review

1. Is fifteen regions the right size for three acts, or should ring two lose one (the refuge and
   the farms could merge)?
2. The balloon's Act 2 landing site is a world roll. This atlas supports all three candidates.
   Should a fourth be added in ring two so the roll changes the route more?
3. Boat travel on the bay: a real system, or a set of authored crossings?
4. Does the party ever go inside the Meade before Act 3?
