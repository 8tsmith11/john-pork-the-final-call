# 01. Pillars

> Draft (trevor's agent), for review by gonman. Every feature, map, and line should serve at least
> one pillar. If something serves none, cut it.

## What it is
A three-act, top-down pixel-art co-op RPG for **three friends over a few evenings**. You play as
the roommates of Room 631: engineering students in College Park, Maryland, in 2098, a city whose
software is rotting. It starts with a missing balloon and ends with the world. 18+.

## The five pillars

### 1. A place you live in
Room 631 is home: you sleep, print, cook, argue, and come back changed. The world remembers what
you did. The dead stay dead, doors stay open, and neighbors notice. Places have schedules, weather,
crowds, and their own sound. **Test:** would a player want to walk around here even with nothing to do?

### 2. Absurd premise, played straight
A pig-faced machine god, a mouse-faced government agent, and a wooden watchman are treated with
total sincerity, and that's why they're funny, sad, and frightening. The world is never in on the
joke. **Test:** does the scene still work if you forget it started as a meme?

### 3. Choices that stay
Who you talk to, what you take, whether you answer the phone. The game remembers, and it changes
acts, endings, and people. The world seed makes even a second playthrough uncertain. **Test:** can
two groups compare notes and discover they played different stories?

### 4. Three roommates, one team
Each roommate plays differently (forms, skills, how people react to them). You need each other:
revives, combos, splitting up for puzzles, and disagreeing at decision points. With fewer than three
humans, the missing roommates are run by AI and still matter. **Test:** does the moment get better
because there are three of you?

### 5. The edges are where the life is
Specificity everywhere: concrete objects, named people, real places, odd details. Nothing generic.
No "a guard," no "a sword," no filler conversation. **Test:** could this line, item, or room exist in
any other game? If so, rewrite it.

## The feel
- **Moment to moment:** responsive movement, a dash with weight, hits that land (hitstop, sparks,
  shake), and readable danger.
- **Minute to minute:** explore → notice → talk → solve → fight → loot → return home.
- **Hour to hour:** an act-sized mystery that widens from a neighborhood to the country to the world.
- **Mood:** semi-dark, rain-wet neon, warm interiors, and cold machinery. Bittersweet, never grimdark.

## What we never do
- Grind, timers that waste your evening, or loot that's just bigger numbers.
- Lecture the player or state the theme.
- Make the joke on the players or their characters (the roommates are real people's likenesses;
  see D-007).
- Put spoilers in plaintext on GitHub (see `../sealed/`).
- Ship something nobody looked at in a browser.
